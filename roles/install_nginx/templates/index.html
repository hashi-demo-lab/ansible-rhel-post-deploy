The reasoning

Why Are They Doing This?

The CA/Browser Forum’s rules govern public CAs — but the reasoning applies to every certificate in your organisation.

Reason 01

Shorter Exposure Window

A compromised key is dangerous for less time. Shorter lifetimes limit the blast radius of any key compromise.

Reason 02

Forces Automation

Human-dependent processes fail at scale. Short lifetimes make automation a requirement, not an option.

Reason 03

Crypto Agility

If an algorithm is broken tomorrow, how quickly can your fleet rotate? Short-lived certs mean faster response.

Reason 04

Reduces Revocation Reliance

CRL and OCSP checking are unreliable in practice. Short-lived certs expire before revocation even matters.

The pattern is clear: public standard today → internal best practice tomorrow. TLS everywhere, encryption at rest, mutual TLS in service meshes — all followed this path. The only question is whether you adopt on your terms or wait for an auditor to ask.

Issue & Deploy

Fleet-wide expiry tracking with configurable thresholds. Proactive, not reactive.

  • Configurable thresholds per environment
  • Compliance-ready reporting on demand
  • Read-only — safe to schedule daily
03 / 05

Renew

Automatic replacement: issue, backup, deploy, verify — zero human intervention.

  • Only renews hosts that need it
  • Backup-first — existing cert preserved
  • Schedulable — run nightly, forget about it
04 / 05

Revoke

Instant revocation through Vault’s PKI engine. Always deliberate, never automated.

  • Takes effect immediately in Vault
  • Every revocation audited with timestamp
  • CRL tidy cleans up expired entries
05 / 05

Rollback

Automatic restore from timestamped backup if deployment or verification fails.

  • Auto-triggered by block/rescue on failure
  • Manual option for emergency recovery
  • TLS-verified after rollback completes
